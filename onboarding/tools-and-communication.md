# Tools & Communication Norms

## Preferred Communication Channels

| Channel | Use For |
|---|---|
| **Microsoft Teams** | Quick coordination, day-to-day discussions, informal updates |
| **Email (Outlook)** | Formal communication, external interactions, important documentation |
| **Meetings (Virtual/In-Person/Hybrid)** | Key decisions, reviews, complex topics, stakeholder alignment |
| **Jira** | Tracking work, documenting progress, maintaining transparency (Product/Tech/QA) |
| **HubSpot (CRM)** | All sales data — leads, pipeline, deals, accounts |
| **Spotdraft** | Legal contract management |

---

## Response Expectations

- **Urgent matters:** Tag relevant stakeholders or connect via call for immediate attention
- **Non-urgent matters:** Use asynchronous communication; allow reasonable response time during working hours
- Respect time zones and working schedules, especially for global teams

---

## Communication Do's

- Be respectful, professional, and constructive in tone
- Keep messages concise and to the point
- Articulate clear context, problem, possible solution, and next steps
- Use the right channel for the right purpose
- Document important decisions in writing
- Share updates proactively and transparently
- Listen actively and encourage open dialogue
- Respect others' time by preparing for meetings
- Reduce meetings wherever possible — a 30-minute meeting with 10 people = 300 minutes of organizational time
- Use **English** as the language of communication
- If teams are not aligned on a topic, bring all stakeholders together to resolve it openly

---

## Communication Don'ts

- Don't use calls or meetings for topics that can be resolved asynchronously
- Don't escalate issues without first attempting resolution through appropriate channels
- Avoid vague messages without context or a clear ask
- Don't overuse "urgent" tags unless truly necessary
- Avoid long, unstructured messages that make it hard to understand the action required
- Don't rely solely on verbal discussions for critical decisions — always document them
- Don't use derogatory language or raised tones in any situation
- Avoid relying on or spreading hearsay or rumors — always verify with facts; connect with appropriate stakeholders for clarity

---

## Decision Making: DRI + RACI

| Model | When to Use |
|---|---|
| **DRI (Directly Responsible Individual)** | Most initiatives and projects — one person accountable for driving the decision/outcome |
| **RACI** (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) | Cross-functional programs and high-risk changes |

**Principle:** Use DRI for speed and clarity; use RACI when coordination is complex.
