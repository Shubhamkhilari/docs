# Loyalife — Loyalty Programs & Qualification

## When Does a Loyalty Program Make Business Sense?

Before discussing a solution, determine if a loyalty program is even right for the prospect.

### Good Fit Indicators

**Industry Characteristics:**
- Commoditized but competitive market — loyalty becomes a differentiation and retention tool
- Industry already has loyalty precedent (competitors run programs)
- Transaction frequency is high enough: daily (banking), weekly (restaurants), monthly (retail/ecom), or quarterly minimum (airlines, hotels)

**Customer Behavior:**
- Fixed total spending in a category — loyalty helps capture more share of that wallet
- Realistic switching opportunities between competitors
- Customers see value in tier progression

**Business Model:**
- Enterprise scale — even a 2% improvement must represent meaningful absolute revenue
- Long-term commitment (2+ years) — loyalty programs take time to show results
- Loyalty viewed as a **strategic initiative**, not just a marketing tactic
- Clear program objective: customer retention/LTV, customer acquisition (CAC), cross-sell/upsell, or behavioral data capture

### Situations Requiring Extra Consideration (Probe Deeper)
- Subscription-only business models
- Purchase frequency > 3–6 months
- Seeking results within 6 months
- Limited scale where improvements won't be meaningful
- No competitive loyalty programs exist in their industry

## Lead Qualification

### Bad Leads
**Signals:**
- Marketing teams exploring various growth options; loyalty is one of many tactics
- Limited competitive loyalty context in their industry
- Initial concept exploration with no concrete plans
- Low loyalty awareness overall
- Unclear program objective
- No urgency

**Sales Perspective:** Require 12+ months nurturing, more educational investment, smaller initial engagements (~$10K/year when they eventually advance)

---

### Good Leads
**Signals:**
- Clear commitment to implementation
- Active vendor evaluation (often formal RFP)
- Defined loyalty budget
- Strategic objectives and metrics identified
- High loyalty awareness
- Detailed requirements documentation
- Cross-functional stakeholder involvement (including decision-makers)
- Defined implementation timeline
- Complex loyalty programs (Loyalife's strength vs cheap DIY tools)

**Sales Perspective:** Predictable sales cycles, 3–9 months, typical revenue $50,000+ USD

---

### Hot Leads (Switching from Existing Solution)
**Signals:**
- Currently using another loyalty solution with specific pain points
- Exploring alternatives due to technology, service, or cost issues
- Very high loyalty awareness
- Specific migration questions
- Well-articulated pain points with current solution
- Technical stakeholder involvement
- Clear expectations based on prior implementation experience

**Sales Perspective:** Most efficient path to conversion, 1–6 months close

## Scale Requirements

Loyalife's value proposition is optimized for **enterprise-scale organizations**.

### Why Scale Matters

- Loyalty programs typically drive single-digit % improvements
  - $1M annual revenue × 2% = $20K impact
  - $1B annual revenue × 2% = $20M impact
- Total program cost = Platform fee + Loyalty rewards budget (rewards budget is typically 100× the platform fee)
  - Example: $50K/yr platform → expect $5M in loyalty rewards budget
- Successful programs require: integration effort, dedicated program management, marketing resources

### "Ready to Buy" Signals
- Live initiative + timeline (<90 days)
- Budget earmarked
- Incumbent pain: slow rule changes, poor segmentation, weak omnichannel
- Data access confirmed (POS/ecom/CRM events)
- IT + InfoSec already engaged
- Triggers: unhappy with current solution, new region launch, M&A program consolidation, churn spike, channel partner performance drop, compliance mandate
